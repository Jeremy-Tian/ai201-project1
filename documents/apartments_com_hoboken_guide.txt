Renting in Hoboken, NJ — Local Guide

Average rent
As of early 2025, the average apartment rent in Hoboken was roughly $2,949 for a studio, $3,719 for a one-bedroom, $4,568 for a two-bedroom, and $6,166 for a three-bedroom. Prices vary a lot by building age and proximity to the waterfront and the PATH. Older walk-ups rent for noticeably less than the doorman buildings on the river.

Broker fees and lease terms
Many Hoboken listings are handled by brokers who charge a fee of one month's rent or more on top of your first month, last month, and security deposit. No-fee buildings exist but tend to be the larger management-company complexes. Standard leases run 12 months; shorter terms are rare and usually priced higher.

What you get for the money
Compared with Manhattan, the same rent buys you more space in Hoboken, and you save on lower state income and sales taxes. Compared with the rest of New Jersey, though, Hoboken is expensive — you're paying a premium for the NYC commute and the walkable, amenity-dense lifestyle.

Parking
Street parking is cheap for residents with a permit but genuinely hard to find. If you have a car, look for a building with a garage or a deeded spot, or budget for a monthly parking lot. Many residents skip the car entirely and rely on PATH, ferry, light rail, and bus.

Walkability
Hoboken scores around 95 on Walk Score, among the most walkable cities in the country. Almost everything — groceries, gyms, restaurants, transit — is within a 10–15 minute walk, which is part of why rents stay high despite the small housing stock.
