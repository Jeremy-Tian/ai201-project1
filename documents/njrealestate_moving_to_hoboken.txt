Moving to Hoboken, NJ: A Guide for New Residents

Hoboken is a small city, just under two square miles, that packs urban energy, waterfront parks, and serious NYC access into a tiny footprint. Because the city is so compact, choosing where to live is less about picking a neighborhood and more about choosing which street you want to be on.

Neighborhoods

Downtown Hoboken, at the south end, is where most of the action is. You're close to the Hoboken Terminal, the PATH train, the ferry, and all the bars and restaurants on Washington Street. It's the most convenient part of the city if you commute into Manhattan daily, but it comes with more foot traffic and weekend noise.

Uptown Hoboken, at the north end, tends to be quieter and more residential. It attracts families and people who want a little more space and a bit less bar noise on weekends. As of mid-2025, average rents uptown ran about $2,958 for a studio, $3,852 for a one-bedroom, $4,776 for a two-bedroom, and $6,469 for a three-bedroom.

The Waterfront, including buildings like Maxwell Place, commands the highest rents — a basic one-bedroom can push past $4,470.

Rental prices

Across Hoboken as a whole, recent averages were roughly $2,949 for a studio, $3,719 for a one-bedroom, $4,568 for a two-bedroom, and $6,166 for a three-bedroom. Rents are the highest in Hoboken itself and lower in the surrounding towns of Jersey City, Union City, and Weehawken. The market moves very quickly, so be ready to act when you find a place you like.

Flooding

One of the biggest drawbacks of living in Hoboken is that it often floods due to its low-lying land. Because the city is so small, there aren't many spots that are safe from floods. Your best bet is to avoid basement and first-floor apartments, which are the most vulnerable when heavy rain overwhelms the drainage system.

Parking

Parking in Hoboken, while cheap for residents, is notoriously hard to find. If you own a car, strongly consider an apartment or condo with private or deeded parking, or be prepared to circle for street parking and rely on the residential permit system.

Commuting and transportation

The PATH train connects Hoboken to Midtown and the Financial District. NY Waterway ferries connect Hoboken to Midtown and Downtown Manhattan and offer a scenic alternative to the rail line. Between PATH, ferry, light rail, and bus service, most residents commute into the city without a car.

Walkability and safety

Hoboken scores a 95 on Walk Score, ranking it among the most walkable places in the country. The town is well known for good housing stock and a low rate of violent crime, making it one of the safer urban areas you'll find.
