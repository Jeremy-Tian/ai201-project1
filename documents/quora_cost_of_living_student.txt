Quora — What is the cost of living for a student in Hoboken, Jersey City, or Union City, NJ?

Answer 1:
All three are expensive by national standards because they're so close to NYC and full of commuters, but they're not equal. Hoboken is the priciest of the three. Jersey City is a notch cheaper overall, though the downtown JC waterfront has caught up to Hoboken prices. Union City and the Heights area of Jersey City are where you actually save money as a student. Expect to spend the bulk of your budget on rent no matter which you pick.

Answer 2:
As a grad student I lived in Union City and commuted into Hoboken. Rent was a few hundred dollars a month less than comparable places in Hoboken, and the bus into Hoboken and the PATH was straightforward. The trade-off is that Union City is less walkable and has fewer of the bars/restaurants that make Hoboken fun. If your priority is saving money, the surrounding towns win.

Answer 3:
Don't forget the upfront costs. In Hoboken you'll often face a broker fee of one month's rent plus first month, last month, and a security deposit — that can be four months of rent before you even move in. Jersey City and Union City have broker fees too, but the lower rent makes the lump sum smaller. Budget for that, not just the monthly number.

Answer 4:
Food and transit are similar across all three. The PATH and buses are the same systems. Groceries are a bit cheaper outside Hoboken. The single biggest variable is rent, so pick your town based on how much rent you can absorb and how much you value walkability versus saving money.
