Hoboken Neighborhood Guide: Where to Live

Hoboken is tiny — under two square miles — so picking where to live is really about picking the right street rather than a sprawling neighborhood. Still, the city breaks down into a few recognizable areas, each with its own trade-offs for renters.

Downtown (South End)
Downtown is the busiest and most convenient part of Hoboken. You're walking distance to the Hoboken Terminal, the PATH, the ferry, and the bars and restaurants along lower Washington Street. It's ideal for daily Manhattan commuters, but you trade quiet for it: expect weekend crowds, late-night street noise, and the highest foot traffic in the city. One-bedrooms downtown tend to sit at or above the citywide average.

Uptown (North End)
Uptown is quieter and more residential. It draws families, couples, and grad students who want a little more calm and slightly more space for the money. There's less nightlife, but you still get grocery stores, parks, and a short walk or bus ride to the PATH. Recent uptown rents averaged about $2,958 for a studio, $3,852 for a one-bedroom, $4,776 for a two-bedroom, and $6,469 for a three-bedroom.

The Waterfront
The waterfront, including newer luxury buildings like Maxwell Place, has the priciest rents in town — a basic one-bedroom can run past $4,470. You're paying for skyline views, modern amenities, and direct access to the waterfront walkway. These buildings tend to have doormen, gyms, and parking, which softens the parking headache that plagues the rest of the city.

Choosing for a commute to Stevens
Because the whole town is walkable and compact, students at Stevens can live almost anywhere in Hoboken and still walk or take a short bus to campus. The bigger question is downtown convenience versus uptown calm versus waterfront amenities — and how much rent you're willing to pay for each.
