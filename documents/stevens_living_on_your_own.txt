Living on Your Own — Off-Campus Housing Guide (Stevens Institute of Technology)

Many Stevens students choose independent housing in Hoboken and the surrounding area. The real estate market moves quickly, so students should be proactive when searching for an apartment.

Nearby cities

Jersey City, North Bergen, Weehawken, Bayonne, Union City, and Secaucus all offer public transit into Hoboken via PATH, light rail, or bus service. Rents in these towns are generally lower than in Hoboken itself, which makes them worth considering for students on a budget who don't mind a short commute.

Finding an apartment

Real estate agents can help with your search but typically charge a broker fee equal to one month's rent or more. Online apartment-finder services let you contact listing agents or owners directly, which can help you avoid realtor fees.

Lease considerations

Only sign a lease when you are certain you want that particular apartment and can commit to the full contract duration, which is usually 12 months. Subletting is permitted only if it is included in your lease agreement. When subletting from an individual rather than a management company, it can become difficult to reach the landlord about maintenance or your security deposit. The Office of Student Life can provide a letter certifying your student status if a landlord requires one.

Before moving in

Get all landlord agreements in writing, including any repairs that were promised. Do a walk-through and document the apartment's condition so you aren't held responsible for existing damage. Establish reliable contact information for your landlord before you move in, and create a roommate agreement with your housemates.

Safety and security

Evaluate the apartment, the building, and the surrounding neighborhood for safety before you sign anything. Visit the area during both day and night hours. Ask realtors and neighbors about local conditions. Verify that you have a safe walking route to campus and a convenient, secure way to commute.
